Recently, a friend asked me for tips on traveling in Vietnam: A friend of his wanted to travel to Vietnam for 10 days (!!). The tips I compiled on this occasion, I've shared at least 5 more times since. That's why I'm writing them down here now. And that's why in dialogue form...

_Hi Till - a young colleague of mine is going to Vietnam with a buddy for 10 days in mid/late November._
_So far, they don't have any concrete plans. Do you have a few tips on what a tourist in Vietnam absolutely must see?_
_Thanks a lot in advance._
_Best regards_

Ugh, that's not easy. It depends on what he likes: city, nature, party, museums...

He will almost certainly land in **Hanoi** or **Saigon**. He should definitely check out the respective city—and probably sleep off the jet lag.

---

In both cities: **EAT!** Street food, typical dishes in each place. Try everything, be brave 😂 Getting around: Grab bike. It's like Uber. Install the app, click on the map, moped comes. You sit on the back and see everything from a Vietnamese perspective. Cars just sit in traffic.

---

In Saigon, I once did a fantastic **Vespa Tour** into the Mekong Delta. A day long, on the back of a Vespa. The tours are also available in Hanoi and Hoi An: [Vespa-Adventures](https://vespaadventuretours.com)

---

**Coffee**: VN is the largest coffee producer in the world. They have a very unique coffee culture. They drink Coffee Phin. A young coffee farmer from the highlands (Da Lat, also very worth seeing) has the coolest coffee shop in Saigon:
[The Workshop Coffee27 Ngô Đức Kế, Bến Nghé, Quận 1, Hồ Chí Minh, Vietnam+84 28 3824 6801](https://goo.gl/maps/uyV7neisEPLL8mgJA)

![Nadine & Binh Coffee](nadine-binh-coffee.JPG)

Remember: The coffee is only authentic if you sit on stools that are way too small at tables that are way too small ;)

---

Nature highlights for us were in the north: **Mountains**, **jungle**, beautiful. Sa Pa is the Chamonix of VN: the town is ugly, but beautiful tours start from there. The [Fansipan](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fansipan), the highest peak of VN, is considered the last peak of the Himalayas. Nowadays, there's even a gondola going up. But it's all quite crowded. We preferred the northeast: fewer tourists, more authentic. The place is called Ha Giang. Best visited by moped, if you dare. If you want to hike there, Panhou Village is a great base camp (you can also get guides there).
[Ecolodge Panhou VillageThông Nguyên, Hoàng Su Phì District, Ha Giang, Vietnam+84 96 661 50 50](https://goo.gl/maps/BwKQgb6QLVN8prxy9)

---

Our area was the beach: Central Vietnam, with **Đà Nẵng** and **Hội An**. Đà Nẵng is Vietnamese, a medium-sized city. Hội An is a UNESCO World Heritage Site, accordingly full of tourists. Besides the typical landmarks from the travel guide, our highlight was **diving**. You can also try it as a non-diver, sort of a taster session. If diving, say you're from Till - then the service will be nicer & cheaper. Every Dive Master responds to the name 😀
[Vietnam Scuba Diving](https://vietnamscubadiving.com)

![Diving](vietnam-diving.jpeg)

---

North of the Cloud Pass is the old imperial city of **Huế**. Very nice - again, the food. Besides the palace, Perfume River, and standard places, the following hotel is highly recommended: luxury from the last century, very nice service, right on the beach...
[Villa Louise Huế - Beach Hotel and Spathôn Hòa Duân, Phú Vang District, Thua Thien Hue 530000, Vietnam+84 91 767 36 56](https://goo.gl/maps/QrQpgRNayjeXdWYS7)
Between Đà Nẵng and Huế, the train is recommended: you see a lot, can chat with the Vietnamese - and buy great food from the mobile vendors at the stations.

---

Further north by train, you reach Đồng Hới: in this area are the **largest caves in the world**. We were there 4 times, visited different caves, some VERY adventurous. Best guides and equipment:
[Oxalis](https://oxalisadventure.com)
